A modular arm, a consistent camera view and a sense of its surroundings. Here’s how the pieces are being designed to help in the garden.

Interchangeable arm attachments

One connection.
A different kind of help.

Swap the tool at the end of the arm to change the job. The latest CAD design pairs a rotating male helical coupler with a matching threaded socket on every attachment. A locking pin holds the connected tool during wrist rotation.

A docking cradle holds each attachment for the proposed screw-on and screw-off exchange. Powered exchanges, electrical contacts and attachment recognition still need hardware and control validation.

A view that stays with the arm.

The camera sits on a raised mount integrated into the front wrist bearing cap, looking down toward the claw or attached tool. The cap stays fixed while the wrist spindle and tool rotate.

How it works Its ribbon cable follows the stationary arm. Each attachment still needs camera and position calibration.

Find a path. Remember a plot.

A wide-angle camera, wheel encoders and forward-facing distance sensors form the planned navigation system for moving between rows and locating plant plots.

How it works Wheel motion estimates travel, distance sensors check the path ahead, and camera observations add scene context. Plot accuracy depends on calibration and garden conditions.

A little more garden context.

Temperature and humidity readings add local environmental context alongside the camera and motion sensors.

How it works The onboard environmental sensor samples the air around the robot. These readings describe local conditions; they don’t measure soil moisture.

The maker behind SpacefruitBuilding a little help for the garden.

What stops people from having their own gardens?

That’s the idea I’m exploring with Spacefruit. I’m building a modular garden robot, starting with the details that make useful work possible: an arm, a camera and tools that can change with the task.

This is a project in progress. I want to share the real process, from CAD and 3D-printed parts to assembly, testing and the changes that come next.

The ambition is simple: technology that makes room for people, plants and a little more possibility.

A few good questions

Before we grow.

Can I buy a Spacefruit robot today?

Spacefruit is in development. The images show a design vision and CAD prototypes, not a shipping product. Campaign details, availability and any delivery commitments belong on the official Kickstarter page.

Will it work alongside people?

That is the design goal: useful help in a garden that still belongs to people. Human interaction, sensing, motion limits and real-world behavior need careful testing as the prototype develops.
